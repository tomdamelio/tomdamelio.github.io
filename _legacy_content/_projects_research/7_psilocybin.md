**Question.** How does psilocybin reshape the dynamics of visual perception and aesthetic experience — and can eye movements index that change?

**What I did.** Co-authored a series of studies on the acute effects of psilocybin: on gaze dynamics during aesthetic perception, on visual salience processing, and on longitudinal neural and psychological change following psilocybin under compassion imagery priming.

**Method.** Eye-tracking to quantitatively assess the effects of low and high doses of psilocybin on eye movements during exploration of complex visual stimuli, focusing on the aesthetic perception of paintings in a double-blind, placebo-controlled design in semi-naturalistic settings; salience modelling; neural, physiological and psychometric assessment at multiple timepoints in the longitudinal work.

**Result.** Higher doses of psilocybin lead to a more localized pattern of visual exploration, producing a less entropic fixation probability distribution. Participants in the high-dose condition also reported an enhanced emotional response and a heightened state of flow, consistent with altered perception of low-level visual information such as textures, shapes and colours. Together the findings support the view that psilocybin significantly affects gaze fixation, mediated by changes in the perception of visual detail.

A separate strand integrates the practice of compassionate imagery with the ceremonial use of psilocybin, using EEG, fMRI, physiological and psychometric assessments to follow psychological and neural change beyond the acute state.

Beyond the specific findings, this line demonstrates the value of studying psychedelics by observing their effects on behaviour in complex, naturalistic conditions — an approach that keeps participants engaged and raises the ecological validity of the results.

**Role and collaborators.** Co-author, with Sophie Müller, Federico Cavanna, Laura de la Fuente, Nicolás Bruno, Carla Pallavicini and Enzo Tagliazucchi.
