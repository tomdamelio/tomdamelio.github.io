**Question.** What are the neural and physiological correlates of moment-to-moment affective experience, when the experience is reported _as it happens_ rather than reconstructed afterwards?

**What I did.** I lead the design and collection of a multimodal dataset in which participants report their emotional state continuously and in real time inside immersive virtual environments, while we record EEG, EOG, EMG, ECG, EDA and respiration simultaneously. The design targets the two weaknesses of existing affective datasets at once: naturalistic, strongly immersive induction, and dense temporal supervision.

**Method.** Real-time continuous annotation in VR; simultaneous central and peripheral recording; analysis of the temporal dynamics of self-reported affect and their neural and physiological correlates; unsupervised methods to surface emergent patterns in those dynamics.

**Why it matters.** Most affective datasets collect a single rating per episode, which confounds the experience with the memory of it and discards the dynamics that make affect interesting. One label per episode is also a starvation diet for any model — and dense supervision is precisely what is missing when it comes to evaluating large pretrained models on affective tasks. By harnessing the immersive power of VR, we aim to capture the full complexity of human emotion in naturalistic settings while keeping the annotation continuous.

**Result.** Pilot data and preliminary analyses presented at ASSC 2025, with a companion line on mapping dynamic emotion and physiology from virtual to real settings presented at ISRE 2024. Dataset and paper in preparation.
